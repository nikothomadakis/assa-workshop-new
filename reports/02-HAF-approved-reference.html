1. Executive assessment

New business is the main reason Protection liabilities increased this quarter, partly offset by claims. On the evidence reviewed, I consider R105,010,319 the appropriate closing balance, an increase of R7,560,383 from R97,449,936. This corrects a R250,000 overstatement in the movement summary.

The initial account needs a broader explanation of the quarter. Lapse experience merits attention, the newest claims cohort is too significant to leave unexplored, and the small net basis change conceals substantial changes in opposite directions. With these matters addressed, I am comfortable with the report’s conclusions, subject to the limitations below.

2. Basis of review

Current materiality: an absolute component is material when it exceeds 10% of absolute net movement OR 1% of opening liability. Using the corrected balances, the thresholds are R756,038.30 and R974,499.36. Exceeding either is sufficient; comparisons are strictly greater-than.

Residual tolerance: 0.5% of opening liability, or R487,249.68. This is not a tolerance for disagreements between sources reporting the same balance.

I have applied the current standard rather than the older investigation thresholds. Expected change remains a baseline. The accompanying human review framework explains the basis for deciding where further evidence is needed and when the findings are sufficient.

Lapse and expense variances

Lapse variance of +R1,431,798 is material. The cohort contributions are +R141,592, +R204,565 and +R1,085,641, respectively, and sum to the total. The newest cohort accounts for 75.8% and is individually material.

With 16 lapses against 34.3 expected, the newest cohort’s A/E is 0.47, compared with approximately 0.94–0.95 for the older cohorts. This is where I would focus the lapse explanation. Fewer exits are consistent with more reserves remaining on the books, but one quarter does not establish a sustained retention improvement. The +R7,221 expense variance does not warrant further investigation on size alone.

6. New business and interpretation

New business contributes +R9,509,035 at recognition. The 97 Q2 entrants have a separate closing liability of R8,654,124. Of these policies, 53 (54.6%) are aged 60+, accounting for R7,132,241 or 82.4% of their closing liability. This concentration is relevant to understanding the business written; the closing balance must not be added to the bridge again.

The Silver Cover campaign is a plausible explanation for the older-age mix. I would retain management’s commentary, but qualify it: the available records contain neither a campaign identifier nor an attribution study, so they do not demonstrate that the campaign caused the growth.

7. Review conclusion and limitations

In my judgement, the corrected report provides a sufficiently supported account of the quarter and is suitable for approval. The closing balance is substantiated, the material experience is covered and the assumption changes are visible. The unexplained residual and limited evidence of campaign causation should remain explicit. Neither prevents approval on the stated basis.
